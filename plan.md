# Plan — Hebrew small models for TTS code-switching

Derived from `transcripts/hebrew-small-models-2026-09-22.md` (voice note, 2026-09-22).
Everything below is the recorded intent plus the structure implied by it. Nothing here
has been validated against prior art yet — the prior-art search is task 1.

## The problem

The *My Weird Prompts* podcast is AI-generated and voiced by TTS (Chatterbox, which
supports per-segment language tagging). Scripts routinely contain Hebrew words written
in Latin characters, because the show is produced in Israel.

The failure mode is graded, not binary:

- Hebrew words that exist in the English corpus — *Shabbat*, *kosher* — are pronounced
  acceptably by an English TTS voice.
- Hebrew words that do not — *Bituach Leumi* (National Insurance), *Mas Hachnasa*
  (Income Tax), *makolet* (corner shop) — are read naively as English grapheme strings.
- Guttural phonemes are the worst case: *challah* comes out as "chala", the ח collapsing
  to an English "ch".

Marking those words with a Hebrew language tag would fix the pronunciation. Nothing in
the pipeline currently identifies them.

## Proposed architecture — three models in series

Each stage is deliberately narrow. Whether three small models in succession is a
reasonable design, rather than one larger model, is an open question (see below).

### 1. Classifier — Hebrew-in-Latin-script detection

Input: a script line. Output: the line annotated with which tokens are Hebrew words
rendered in Latin characters.

> "I'm going to Bituach Leumi today" → `Bituach` positive, `Leumi` positive.

Payload shape (boolean per token, or spans) is undecided. This is the model Daniel
considers worth building regardless of the rest: useful across future projects, and
tolerable even at imperfect accuracy.

**Prior art:** a search for language-identification small models returned candidates,
none of which covered Hebrew. Treat that as preliminary, not conclusive.

**Training data — three candidate routes:**

1. A lexicon of Hebrew words in Latin transliteration. May not exist in usable form.
2. Harvest the existing Whisper code-switching fine-tune dataset: a few hundred
   read sentences, each generated by an LLM under the instruction *"generate sentences
   the user might speak which contain at least one Hebrew word in a natural context"*,
   then recorded by Daniel. Published on Hugging Face.
   **Action: locate and record the exact dataset ID here.**
3. Fresh synthetic data — large-model generation, large-model annotation, then manual
   review. Reviewing ~1,000 annotations in a basic GUI is considered fast enough to be
   practical.

Route 2 or 3 is likelier than 1. Possibly a fine-tune of an existing model rather than
training from scratch; that decision comes after the prior-art search.

### 2. Transliteration — Latin → Hebrew script

Input: a token flagged by stage 1. Output: the same word in Hebrew characters.
`Shabbat` → `שבת`.

Daniel is confident models for this exist and has seen at least one. The unknowns are
what the task is conventionally called (most published work runs the other direction,
Hebrew → English) and whether any candidate is **open source and open weight**.

**This stage is a search problem, not a build problem** — scope it as evaluation.

### 3. Script normalisation for TTS

Rewrite the script into Chatterbox's tagged form: English as the ambient language tag
throughout, with each identified Hebrew word replaced by its Hebrew-script form and
wrapped in Hebrew language markers.

Whether this needs a model at all, or is a deterministic templating step over stage 1
and 2 output, is unresolved — it is listed as a possible third model but has no
learned component described.

## Downstream pipeline consequences

The podcast currently generates one clip per speaker turn, then a concatenation script
assembles all clips plus show elements into a single normalised MP3.

Code-switching breaks that assumption: a single turn containing a Hebrew word needs
**multiple clips with different language parameters**, concatenated within the turn.
Expect to need padding between sub-clips so the joins do not read as audible gaps.
Tuning that is a listen-and-adjust job, not something to design up front.

## Open questions

1. Does a Hebrew-in-Latin-script classifier already exist? (Initial search: no.)
2. Is there an open-weight Latin → Hebrew transliteration model?
3. Is chaining three small models per script a sound design, or over-decomposition?
   Daniel has not experimented with small language models and flags this as unknown.
4. Does a Latin-transliteration Hebrew lexicon exist, or is synthetic data the route?
5. What is the exact Hugging Face ID of the Whisper code-switching fine-tune dataset?
6. Is the whole chain worth the effort relative to the gain? Explicitly acknowledged as
   possibly not — the classifier alone is judged worth it either way.

## Scope decision

**In scope for this project:** build the classifier (stage 1) if prior art does not
already provide it; find out what exists for stage 2.

Stage 3 and the audio-assembly changes are downstream work, recorded here so the
architecture is complete, not committed to.
